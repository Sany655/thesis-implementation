LITERATURE REVIEW
2.1 Image Processing Techniques
Early research on automated nail disease detection mainly focused on traditional image processing techniques. These methods aimed to analyze visible characteristics of nails, such as color variation, texture patterns, and structural changes, to identify abnormalities associated with different diseases. Due to their low computational requirements, these approaches were initially considered suitable for basic diagnostic support systems.

2.3 Deep Learning and CNN-Based Detection
The introduction of deep learning, particularly Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), significantly advanced the field of nail disease detection by enabling automatic feature extraction from raw images. Unlike traditional machine learning approaches, CNN-based methods eliminate the need for handcrafted features and are capable of learning complex spatial and texture patterns directly from nail images.

Despite their strong performance, CNN-based nail disease detection systems face challenges related to overfitting, dataset imbalance, and deployment constraints. Many deep models are computationally intensive and not optimized for mobile healthcare environments. These limitations highlight the need for efficient architectures and deployment-aware system design.
